# Story Specification: *The Last Memory*

This is the narrative for **TileVania — Treasure Hunt**. It is inspired by the torment of Tantalus: something you want badly stays within reach, and you can never hold it. The myth itself is not retold. The game keeps its psychological core: **the hero cannot stop reaching for the memory, and cannot bear to hold it.**

---

## 1. The truth (what really happened)

*The player is never told all of this directly. It is the hidden logic that every clue follows.*

1. **Before the game.** The hero lived with their little sister, **Lia**, in a house at the edge of the forest. They were poor. The hero heard about the gold in the old castle's treasure chamber.
2. **The promise.** One night the hero left to steal it. They told Lia: *"I'll be back by morning, with enough gold for both of us."* She put a lantern in the window so they could find their way home.
3. **The loss.** Lia was afraid for them. She took the lantern and followed them into the forest. She called out *"Wait for me!"*, but the hero was in a hurry and didn't turn around. The hero came home at dawn. The house was empty, and Lia was never found.
4. **The bargain.** The guilt was more than the hero could bear. They went back to the treasure chamber. Its creature, **the Keeper**, does not hoard gold. It keeps the things people cannot carry. The hero gave it the memory of Lia, and begged:
   > *"Keep it. Even if I come back and beg for it, don't give it back to me."*

   The Keeper promised.
5. **Why everything else went too.** Lia was woven through every part of the hero's life: home, name, childhood. Taking her out unravelled everything. The hero's memories became the scattered **gems**.
6. **The cycle.**
   - Forgetting doesn't remove the *shape* of what is missing. The hero feels a hole and is pulled toward the treasure chamber without knowing why.
   - The Keeper fights them every time, because it promised to.
   - When the hero wins and opens the chest, the memory comes back: her voice, her lantern, almost her name. And with it comes the first edge of the grief.
   - The part of the hero that made the bargain flinches, and the wish to forget repeats itself. **The hero is the one who erases the memory, every time.** There is no outside curse.
7. **The fading self.** Forgetting is not instant. As the hero's mind empties, their fading self stumbles back the way it came:
   - It scratches a tally and *"I was so close."* beside the treasure door.
   - It carves one more notch into the old tree in the forest, where Lia was lost.
   - Long ago, a fading self pinned a note to that tree: *"This time, don't open the chest."*

   Then the hero falls asleep under the tree and wakes up with nothing.
8. **The count.** There were **seven** cycles before the player's first game. Each completed game adds one more notch. The count is saved between play sessions.

### The rules the story obeys
- **Gems are fragments of memory.** When the chest opens, the hoard bursts and scatters across the forest and the castle. That is why gems are everywhere, and why the hero collects them again each cycle.
- **The dragon is not evil.** It keeps a promise the hero forced on it. It knows the cycle and is tired of it, but it will not break its word. It cannot give the memory back. It also cannot stop the hero from taking it once it has been beaten.
- **The cycle could end.** The hero would have to choose to keep the memory, grief included. The game doesn't show this. The final line leaves that hope open, which keeps the ending haunting rather than hopeless.
- **No narrator, no companion.** Every line belongs to the hero (`???`), the dragon, a ghost, a written note, or a remembered voice.

## 2. What the player experiences

The table maps the in-game chapters onto the three levels. The level names are unchanged.

| Level | Chapter subtitle | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| Level 1: The Whispering Forest | **The Awakening** | Amnesia, the note, the notches, gems as memories |
| Level 2: The Abandoned Castle | **The Forgotten Past** | Déjà vu, a ghost who recognises the hero, proof they reached the door before |
| Level 3: The Treasure Chamber | **The Last Memory** | The Keeper, the chest, the loss, the loop |

The hero's speaker name is **`???`** throughout: they don't know their own name.

### 2.1 Forest: The Awakening
- **Opening (cinematic, about 15 s).** The screen is black with letterbox bars. A distant voice says *"...wait for me..."*. The view fades in on the hero lying under an old tree. They get up.
  - ???: *Where... am I?*
  - ???: *I can't remember anything. Not even my name.*
  - Then the title card appears, and the player has control.
- **The Notched Tree** (three steps to the right, automatic, must be seen). A note is pinned to the trunk below carved tally marks.
  - Note: *This time, don't open the chest.*
  - ???: *What chest? ...The handwriting looks familiar.*
  - ???: *Someone has carved notches into this tree. Seven of them.* (The number matches the actual notch count.)
  - ???: *Some are old and grown over. The last one is fresh.*
- **First gem** (a short line that doesn't stop the game):
  - *(It's warm. For a heartbeat I heard someone laughing.)*
  - *(As if it holds a piece of something I've lost.)*
- **The Rusted Lantern** (optional, press **C** to examine, by the ruined gateway).
  - *A rusted lantern lies half-buried in the moss. Someone carried it a long way.*
  - ???: *Why does looking at it hurt?*
- **Memory fragments** (optional glowing shards, two in the forest): see §3.

### 2.2 Castle: The Forgotten Past
- **Déjà vu** (entrance, a short line that doesn't stop the game): *(I know this hall. I know where every step goes before I take it.)*
- **The Faint Ghost** (a harmless, translucent ghost after the broken bridge; automatic):
  - Faint Ghost: *Back again?*
  - ???: *You know me? Then tell me who I am.*
  - Faint Ghost: *Nobody here remembers that anymore. Not even you.*
  - Faint Ghost: *You always rush past us, as if someone is waiting for you.*
  - Then it fades away.
- **The Treasure Door** (automatic). Scratched into the wall: ***"I was so close."*** with a tally of marks.
  - ???: *"I was so close." ...It's the same handwriting as the note.*
  - ???: *And the same marks as the tree. Seven of them.*
  - ???: *I've stood at this door before. Seven times.*
  - This reveals that the hero has reached the treasure chamber before, without explaining why.
- **Memory fragments:** two in the castle.

### 2.3 Treasure Chamber: The Last Memory
- **Before the fight.** The portcullis falls and the dragon swoops in. The world is frozen during this conversation.
  - Dragon: *So. You've come back.*
  - ???: *You know me?*
  - Dragon: *Better than you know yourself. You know how this ends.*
  - ???: *Then why am I here?*
  - Dragon: *Because you never remember the answer.*
  - Dragon: *I made you a promise once. I will keep it again.*
  - The boss bar reads **The Keeper**.
- **On retry** after dying, there is a short line that doesn't stop the game: Dragon: *Again, then.*
- **After the dragon is defeated** (frozen), the dragon fades away and the gold chest appears.
  - Dragon: *Enough... You have won. You always do.*
  - ???: *What's inside that chest?*
  - Dragon: *What you gave me to keep. What you begged me never to give back.*
  - Dragon: *Take it, if you can bear it. You never can.*
- **The chest (ending).**
  1. The lid opens and gold light pours out. The music stops.
  2. A small silhouette holding a lit lantern appears in the light.
  3. The voice and the hero speak:
     - A small voice: *You said you'd be home by morning.*
     - ???: *I know you... I know you!*
     - A small voice: *I kept the lantern burning, so you could find your way home.*
     - The hero steps forward. ???: *Your name is... your name is—*
     - If all four memory fragments were found, the hero gets one syllable further: *Your name is... Li—*
  4. The silhouette dissolves. The chest's gems burst outward and scatter, and the HUD gem counter drains to zero. Everything fades to white.
     - ???: *No... not again...*
  5. On black, there is one soft knock, like a knife cutting bark.
     - ???: *(Don't... open... the chest...)*

### 2.4 The loop
The game returns to the forest and **the opening plays again, word for word**. Then:
1. The hero walks to the tree. The newest notch (now **eight**) glows faintly.
2. Note: *This time, don't open the chest.*
3. ???: *What chest?*
4. Fade to black. Final line, centred: ***Somewhere, a lantern is still burning.***
5. The ending panel appears:
   - Title: **The Last Memory**. Subtitle: *The cycle begins again.*
   - Stats: gems, memories glimpsed (x of 4), time, deaths, notches on the tree.
   - Buttons: **Begin Again** (keep playing the new cycle from the forest) or **Main Menu**.

The hero doesn't know there used to be seven notches; the player does. That gap is the ending's sting.

## 3. Memory fragments (optional)

Memory fragments are pale, glowing shards placed off the main path. Picking one up briefly freezes the world and shows one remembered line plus the hero's reaction. Each shard is also worth 3 gems. The ending panel counts how many were found.

| # | Where | Memory | Hero |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Forest: the floating ledge east of the ruins | *A small house at the edge of a forest. A lantern in the window.* | *Home? Was it mine?* |
| 2 | Forest: the high surface east of the cave entrance | A distant voice: *Wait for me! You're walking too fast!* | *A child's voice. I know it. Why can't I see her face?* |
| 3 | Castle: the top of the watchtower, left of the key | *"I'll be back by morning. With enough gold for both of us."* | *I said that. But to whom?* |
| 4 | Castle: past the exit portal, at the far east wall | *Calling a name through the trees until my voice was gone.* | *...What name?* |

None of them reveals the full truth. Together they set up the ending: the house, the lantern, a child, a promise about gold, a search.

## 4. How the ending recontextualises the clues

| Clue | First reading | After the ending |
|---|---|---|
| Gems | Treasure; "warm", "a piece of something lost" | The hero's own memories, scattered when the chest opened |
| The note | A cryptic warning from a stranger | Written by the hero's fading self, who knew what the chest does |
| The notches | Someone keeps coming back | The hero, once per cycle. The player watches one get added. |
| "I was so close." | A previous adventurer failed | The hero, walking back as they forgot |
| The lantern in the moss | Junk | Lia's lantern, lost where she was lost |
| "...wait for me..." | A dream | The last thing Lia said to the hero |
| The ghost: "as if someone is waiting for you" | Atmosphere | Lia, and the lantern in the window |
| The dragon: "I made you a promise" | Villain talk | The hero's own request: never give it back |
| "Somewhere, a lantern is still burning." | — | Hope: the memory, and perhaps Lia, still wait for them |

## 5. Narrative decisions (made autonomously, open to review)
- **The sister, "Lia".** A missing sibling gives the strongest emotional reason to both want and fear a memory, and it stays suitable for students. Her fate is deliberately left as *lost, never found*. She is not stated to be dead.
- **The dragon is renamed "The Keeper"** in the boss bar and messages. It is still the same boss with the same mechanics.
- **The hero erases their own memory.** This replaces a magical seal or curse and keeps the Tantalus idea psychological.
- **The notch count is saved between play sessions** in PlayerPrefs. It starts at 7, and each finished game adds one. It is a small meta touch: the tree remembers how often *you* have played.
- **"Begin Again" continues the new cycle.** The game doesn't send the player back to the menu, so the loop is literal.
- **The note-and-tree beat is mandatory** (an automatic trigger next to the spawn). The lantern, the shards and the end-of-castle tally reward attention.

## 6. Interaction and pacing rules
- Dialogue is short. No conversation is longer than six lines, and blocking dialogues freeze the world, so enemies and projectiles can't hurt the hero while text is on screen.
- Barks (thought lines that don't stop the game) never take control away.
- **Keys:**
  - **Z / Space / X / Enter** advance dialogue. The first press finishes the typewriter, the next one advances.
  - **C** or **Enter** examines objects.
  - **Esc** is disabled during dialogue and cutscenes.
- Each story beat fires **once per cycle**, and some beats require earlier ones:
  - The boss defeat line needs the boss intro.
  - The ending needs the defeat.
  - Shards already found this cycle don't respawn.
- Story state resets only at the start of a new cycle (Play from the menu, or the loop).
